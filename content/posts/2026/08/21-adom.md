# Ancient Domains of Mystery

_Ancient Domains of Mystery_ is the worst game I've ever played.

You might ask why I'm playing it.

Simple; I played it a lot back in the late '90's and early '00's, and I've recently been working on yet another roguelike, and I thought I might as well go back and play some of the classics I played back then (like _NetHack_, _Angband_, etc), some I hadn't (_Brogue_, _Dwarf Fortress_, _Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup_, _Hauberk_), rip off some of their ideas, see where I think they made missteps, etc.

To summarize: I don't know who needs to hear this, but the vast majority of roguelike developers are not brilliant engineers designing a meticulous simulacrum of a vibrant fantasy world, trading polished marketing and glitzy graphics for boundless possibility and elegant mechanics. They are autistic men who know nothing about game design, obsessed with trivial details, who should probably be under someone's care.

[We can smell our own.]

As I knew nothing of game design at the time (and was apparently incapable of thinking through the basics myself), _ADoM_'s crushing difficulty and general bizarreness didn't shock me.

I had grown up with Nintendo games, where it was more or less expected that you would just suffer through whatever you had bought with $40 or so of your parents' hard-earned 1989 dollars. And on school trips to the mall (I didn't go to a very good school), you'd go to the arcade - which was filled with machines explicitly designed to keep you pumping in quarter after quarter until your inverted jeans pockets held nothing but lint and shame.

But enough about me; let's discuss _Ancient Domains of Mystery_.

![Ancient Domains of Mystery](/images/2026-08-21-adom-001.png)

The first thing you should know is that I'm playing the free (as in beer, not as in freedom or puppies) ASCII version, which is what I played back in the day. There's also a version with graphical tiles, sound, etc. I don't know if it's any good. I'm just here to shit on a freeware game because, honestly, it deserves it.

![Choose a race for your character](/images/2026-08-21-adom-002.png)

You have a few screens to pick attributes about your character - their sex, race, and class. _ADoM_ gives a lot of choices here, which is nice, and the manual's pretty good about describing the costs and benefits of each. Unlike in some other games I've played, the tradeoffs are significant. Your choices make the difference between being doomed in one way or being doomed in another, different way.

For this "review", I'm going to go ahead and go with one of the recommended builds: a high elven archer for "a strong ranged fighter".

![My character's biography](/images/2026-08-21-adom-003.png)

One of the nice things about _ADoM_ is that it generates a background for you - some astrological stuff to give you some nice bonuses, and a background to help you sympathize with and form an attachment to the character that is statistically likely to die a permadeath in the next 5-10 minutes.

Random bonuses are nice, but I think this could be improved. Instead of an astrological basis, which just seems arbitrary, I think it would be better if the biography were used to generate the bonuses, or vice versa. In this case, **I am literally an archer**; it would not be weird to have a military background, including perhaps some tactical training.

But the biography seems only to be there to give information about the character - okay, I'm wealthy, I like legends, I was a natural leader, and... I had to finance my apprenticeship? But I'm wealthy. Aren't apprentices normally paid, if a pittance? And if I'm 195, how long was this apprenticeship?

![Picking a talent](/images/2026-08-21-adom-004.png)

After that, you get to pick a talent, which is another source of bonuses. Being an archer, I went with "Good Shot" (+1 to hit, +1 to damage with ranged weapons).

![Welcome to the Drakalor Chain](/images/2026-08-21-adom-009.png)

So you're dumped outside. Which is great! I love there being an "Overworld", with nature. Snow-capped peaks! A brown-ish path! We'll see trees and water eventually, too. Can't see shit right now.

Our attributes are visible. **Strength**, **Learning**, **Wisdom**, **Dexterity**, **Toughness**, **Charisma**, **Appearance**, **Mana**, and **Perception**. These are basically the D&D stats plus physical appearance (how hot you are), mana (how lucky/magically attuned you are), and perception (vision, hearing, attention to detail).

The **L** is for "Lawful," signifying that I'm on the side of everything that is good and true, basically. The other major options are **N** for "Neutral" and **C** for "Chaotic", which is bad and selfish.

The **DV** and **PV** represent "Defensive Value" and "Protection Value" and indicate your ability to avoid suffering damage and to minimize the damage suffered. **H** are "Hit Points", **P** are "Power Points" (used for magic), **Exp** for "Experience" (level/XP), a location code ("DrCh" means "Drakalor Chain"), and **Sp** for "Speed". This indicates how frequently you can act. With a speed of 102, I can act 102 times for every 100 times that a creature with speed 100 could act.

Now might be a good time to check our inventory:

![My Inventory](/images/2026-08-21-adom-010.png)

Elven chain mail (nice, seems more congruent with the "wealthy family" narrative than the "struggling apprentice" bit), a dagger, a long bow, and... 11 (eleven) arrows. Eleven arrows? For an archer? Ah, but we have the fletchery set.

Here, stones is a unit of weight, so notice that with my basic equipment, I'm already carrying 1/3 of my capacity. Does not bode well.

Back to the map - the little Claude logo there is a cave. Being genre-savvy, I'll enter it.

![An Unassuming Cave](/images/2026-08-21-adom-011.png)

So we're dropped into a cave with no apparent way to progress.

Again, being genre-savvy, I'll **[s]earch** along the walls, at which point a door will appear.

![An Unassuming Door](/images/2026-08-21-adom-012.png)

That's cool! Surely, a secret door will have something cool behind it.

![A New Friend!](/images/2026-08-21-adom-013.png)

Oh, a goblin. How long have you been trapped behind a secret door in this cave, goblin? Don't worry, I'll help you escape!

...And they're hostile.

I'm not going to give a full blow-by-blow here. This is a classic roguelike: the bad guys are letters, the tactics are fairly basic, and you might be able to get some loot off their corpses.

![One-Shotted the Goblin](/images/2026-08-21-adom-014.png)

But the next bit is fairly important, because this is where the real issues with the design of this game start to emerge.

![A Rat Corpse](/images/2026-08-21-adom-015.png)
![Tasty!](/images/2026-08-21-adom-016.png)

I fight a giant rat, overcome it, and eat it raw. High elves, in Ancardia, are built a little different from the elves you might've encountered in other fantasy games. But that rather surprising culinary experience isn't the whole issue; I'll come back to this later.
