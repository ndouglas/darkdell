# Ancient Domains of Mystery

_Ancient Domains of Mystery_ is the worst game I've ever played.

You might ask why I'm playing it. Simple; I played it a lot back in the late '90's and early '00's, and I've recently been working on yet another roguelike, and I thought I might as well go back and play some of the classics I played back then (like _NetHack_, _Angband_, etc), some I hadn't (_Brogue_, _Dwarf Fortress_, _Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup_, _Hauberk_), rip off some of their ideas, see where I think they made missteps, etc.

To summarize: I don't know who needs to hear this, but the vast majority of roguelike developers are not brilliant engineers designing a meticulous simulacrum of a vibrant fantasy world, trading polished marketing and glitzy graphics for boundless possibility and elegant mechanics. They are autistic men who know nothing about game design, obsessed with trivial details, who should probably be under someone's care.

As I knew nothing of game design at the time (and was apparently incapable of thinking through the basics myself), _ADoM_'s crushing difficulty and general bizarreness didn't shock me. I had grown up with Nintendo games, where it was more or less expected that you would just suffer through whatever you had bought with $40 or so of your parents' hard-earned 1989 dollars. And on school trips to the mall (I didn't go to a very good school), you'd go to the arcade - which was filled with machines explicitly designed to keep you pumping in quarter after quarter until your inverted jeans pockets held nothing but lint and shame.

But enough about me; let's discuss _Ancient Domains of Mystery_.
